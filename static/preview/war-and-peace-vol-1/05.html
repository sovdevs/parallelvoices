Kutuzov

Russian Field Marshal and Commander-in-Chief · 189 mentions, 34 spoken lines

Kutuzov is the weary, elderly Commander-in-Chief, physically frail yet strategically perceptive. He speaks with a weak, trembling voice or loud clarity to emphasize points, often yawning or closing his eyes in apparent indifference. He treats subordinates like Prince Andrei with paternal warmth, dismissing their faults gently, while showing impatience toward incompetent generals. He acknowledges military reality over protocol, admitting defeat beforehand and rejecting ceremonial displays. His demeanor shifts from cynical resignation to sudden, sharp authority when witnessing soldierly spirit, revealing a deep, unspoken connection to the troops' morale despite his outward exhaustion.

Prince Andrei Bolkonsky

Prince Andrei, husband of the little princess and Pierre's friend · 503 mentions, 179 spoken lines

Prince Andrei is a pragmatic, duty-bound officer who despises superficiality. He treats subordinates with polite disdain and speaks bluntly, often cutting off conversations abruptly. He values military competence, praising Captain Tushin’s heroism and Napoleon’s genius while mocking political chatter. Emotionally distant from his wife, whom he neglects, yet deeply concerned for his sister’s well-being, he reveals inner conflict about his life’s purpose. His behavior oscillates between cynical detachment and sudden, intense action, driven by an internal sense of necessity rather than external validation.

Nikolai Rostov

young officer and protagonist who loses his purse · 426 mentions, 80 spoken lines

Nikolai Rostov is an enthusiastic, patriotic hussar who speaks with emotional intensity and baritone warmth. He displays fierce loyalty to Tsar Alexander I and camaraderie with comrades like Denisov, often sharing drinks and stories of bravery at Schengraben. Proud and impulsive, he refuses to apologize for calling a colonel a liar, valuing personal honor over rank. Rostov exhibits deep affection for Sonya, showing concern and protectiveness. His behavior oscillates between boisterous military joviality and serious battlefield focus, revealing a character driven by passion, duty, and strong interpersonal bonds.

Dolokhov

officer making a bet to drink rum from window · 74 mentions, 32 spoken lines

Dolokhov is a reckless, defiant officer once demoted for misconduct. He engages in dangerous wagers, such as drinking rum while perched on a window ledge without holding on. In battle, he exhibits fierce courage and aggression, shouting orders to his men and attacking enemies with ferocity. Despite being wounded, he leads his remaining soldiers into combat. His behavior is characterized by boldness, contempt for danger, and a volatile nature, often clashing with others through insults or challenges.

Denisov

fellow officer and friend of Ростов · 88 mentions, 44 spoken lines

Denisov, or Vaska Denisov, is a rotmister of the Hussars, known for his intense passion and rough demeanor. He speaks with a distinctive lisp, often using coarse language and exclamations like 'God damn it.' Emotionally volatile, he oscillates between drunken despair over lost cards and fierce admiration for comrades' bravery. He treats subordinates harshly yet shares intimate moments with them, such as drinking with Rostov. His actions are impulsive; he smashes pipes in anger and rides recklessly, embodying a wild, untamed spirit that contrasts with formal military decorum.

Prince Bagration

Russian general leading the vanguard detachment · 109 mentions, 19 spoken lines

Prince Bagration is a brave, weary general commanding an exhausted detachment. He speaks with quiet authority, often reassuring subordinates like Prince Andrei or dismissing trivialities. Observant yet detached, his 'sleepy eyes' miss nothing, leading him to assign tasks based on merit rather than rank; he values practical bravery over staff pretensions. His demeanor is calm and decisive, moving with the awkward gait of a cavalryman toward danger. He prioritizes saving his army, using truces strategically, and treats officers who prove themselves as useful assets, fostering loyalty through competence and understated leadership.

Captain Tushin

small artillery captain without boots at the market tent · 72 mentions, 19 spoken lines

Captain Tushin is a small, hunched artillery officer who endures isolation and exhaustion on the battlefield. He speaks with gentle deference, addressing superiors as 'Your Highness' while treating soldiers warmly as 'dear friends.' Despite his awkwardness and lack of boots, he remains humble and hardworking, caring deeply for his men. His demeanor shifts between shy embarrassment and quiet dignity, earning him respect from Prince Andrei and Bagration despite initial skepticism about his condition.
